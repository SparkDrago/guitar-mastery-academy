# Phase 1 — Source Analysis and Knowledge Map

Guitar Mastery Academy treats four supplied books as its primary educational references. This document records what each book actually contains, what it does not, how the books overlap, and how their concepts map into the academy curriculum. All lesson text in the app is original writing; nothing here or in the app reproduces the books' prose, exercises, song transcriptions or illustrations.

### Limitations — stated plainly

1. **The brief describes "three Hal Leonard Electric Guitar Theory books." The supplied set is different**: an *acoustic* Book 1, a *songbook* for Book 2, and the electric *Book 3*. None of them is a theory book; all are tab-first playing methods.
2. **The Book 2 method is missing.** Book 3 repeatedly says "you learned this in Book Two" for: the major scale, scale degrees, dominant seventh chords and their barre shapes, the shuffle feel, sixteenth notes, power chords, palm muting, string bends, vibrato, and the minor pentatonic. Those topics are therefore taught in the academy from **established pedagogy (supplementary material)** rather than from a supplied source, and are labelled that way.
3. The two Hal Leonard PDFs are scans with **no text layer**. They were read visually, page by page.
4. All four sources together stop at roughly an **early-intermediate** level: barre chords, the minor scale, 12-bar and minor blues, add/sus and minor seventh chords, and diatonic 3NPS scales and modes. Everything from Level 6 onward in the academy (harmonic and melodic minor, symmetrical scales, advanced harmony, every shred technique, improvisation pedagogy, ear training, jamming) is **supplementary**.

## 2. Book-by-book concept inventory

### A. Acoustic Guitar Tab Method, Book 1

Teaching order as it appears in the book:

1. **Orientation**: parts of the guitar; standard tuning E–A–D–G–B–E; which way a tuning key raises or lowers pitch; using an electronic tuner; sitting and standing posture; fret-hand finger numbers 1–4, arched fingers, fingertips, thumb behind the neck; holding the pick between thumb and index finger.
2. **Tablature basics**: lines are strings, numbers are frets, 0 = open; stacked numbers sound together.
3. **First open chords and strumming**: Em, then C (sixth string not played), G, D; later Am, Dm and two fingerings of A. Common-finger pivots between chords; down-up strumming; staccato by palm-damping; reading chord charts instead of strum-by-strum tab.
4. **Notes on each string in the first five frets**, one string at a time from low E to high E, each paired with riffs on that string.
5. **Alternate picking** (down/up strokes) for single-note lines.
6. **Rhythm tab**: bar lines, measures, time signatures (4/4, 3/4); quarter, half, eighth and whole notes; quarter, half, eighth and whole rests; ties; pickup notes; accents; repeat signs, first/second endings.
7. **Muting** a rest with the pick-hand palm or by releasing fret pressure.
8. **Double stops** (dyads); "let ring."
9. **Legato**: slides, hammer-ons, pull-offs, slurs; bluegrass/Celtic scalar runs.
10. **Arpeggios**: ascending and descending broken chords, string skipping, a descending bass line under a held chord, mixing arpeggios with strums.

Strengths: very gradual, song-motivated, excellent rhythm-reading sequence. Gaps: no theory of *why* chords contain their notes; no fretboard beyond fret 5; acoustic set-up only (no amp, pickups, electric technique).

### B. Guitar Tab Method, Songbook 2

Ten complete songs (rock, blues, pop, alt-rock, hard rock). Techniques exercised across them: power chords (including slash power chords such as C5/E), palm muting and "slight P.M.", sixteenth-note rhythm figures, shuffle feel, bends (½, full) and vibrato in solos, slides, let-ring arpeggios, chord strumming in pop songs, and navigating a chart (rhythm figures, repeats, endings, D.S./D.S.S. al Coda, codas, fades).

Role in the academy: **repertoire evidence**. It shows which techniques a Book 2 student is expected to use in real songs. The academy does not reproduce these transcriptions; instead it uses the technique list to shape Level 1, 4 and 5 exercises and the "chart reading" lesson.

### C. Guitar Tab Method, Book 3

| Book 3 section | Concepts |
|---|---|
| Barre chords | Chord-diagram reading; E-shape major/minor barre chords (root on string 6); A-shape major/minor (root on string 5, including the ring-finger "double barre"); E- and A-shape dominant seventh barres; double-stop bend; 5/4, 6/8 and changing meters in riffs; muted strums ("x"); arpeggio riffs |
| The minor scale | Natural minor formula; one-string version; two moveable shapes rooted on strings 6 and 5; trills; 7/4 meter; diatonic thirds; power chords drawn from the scale |
| Music Theory 201 | **Relative minor** (same notes, start on degree 6; slide down three frets from the major root); **diatonic chord numbers** (I ii iii IV V vi vii°); the **Nashville number system** for key-independent progressions |
| 12-bar blues | Standard and quick-change forms with I7, IV7, V7; slash rhythm notation; **blues scale** = minor pentatonic + ♭5; shuffle feel; sixteenth-note triplets; call-and-response; **turnarounds**; hybrid picking; ninth chords as blues substitutes; stop-time |
| 12/8 time | Compound meter counted in four groups of three; slow blues; moveable seventh-chord shapes |
| Add & sus chords | Triads defined as root–3rd–5th; add9 and add4; **sus2 and sus4** (replace the 3rd); sus resolution; sus2 barre and the moveable root–5th–2nd voicing; sus4 barre; Drop D tuning; **pre-bends** |
| Minor seventh chords | Formula 1–♭3–5–♭7; open Am7, Dm7, Em7 fingerings; slash chords; common tones; capo; m7 barres on strings 6 and 5; m11 as an extension |
| Minor blues | 12-bar form with m7 on i and iv, dominant or minor v; arpeggiated minor-triad soloing |

Strengths: ties every concept to recognisable rock and blues riffs; first explicit theory (relative minor, chord numbers). Gaps: no interval vocabulary, no explanation of *why* diatonic chords are major or minor, no full-neck view of the minor scale, no improvisation method.

### D. Pillitiere, *Building the Better Guitar Scale*

The book's central claims:

* Learn **one** scale system, the major scale, and all natural diatonic scales and modes follow. Relative major and minor share notes and patterns ("two scales out of one pattern").
* Play scales **three notes per string (3NPS)** for left-hand consistency and a predictable right-hand pattern; Pillitiere's own suggestion is classical i–m alternation, and the academy also offers a pick version.
* The seven 3NPS patterns are built from only **three per-string finger shapes**:
  * **X**: whole–whole (fingers 1-2-4, spanning 5 frets)
  * **Y**: half–whole (1-2-4, spanning 4 frets)
  * **Z**: whole–half (1-3-4, spanning 4 frets)
* On an imaginary infinite-string guitar the shapes repeat in a fixed **7-string cycle: X X X Y Y Z Z**. A six-string guitar always shows a window onto that cycle.
* **Two vertical rules** place the shapes on the frets:
  1. moving from an X string to a Y string, the first finger moves up one fret;
  2. crossing from the 3rd (G) string to the 2nd (B) string, the first finger moves up one fret. The two shifts add up when both happen together.
* To build any pattern you need only **the starting scale degree and the first two shapes**: 1 → XX, 2 → ZX, 3 → YZ, 4 → XY, 5 → XXX, 6 → ZZ, 7 → YY.
* The same seven shape-starts are the **seven modes** (XX Ionian, ZX Dorian, YZ Phrygian, XY Lydian, XXX Mixolydian, ZZ Aeolian, YY Locrian), and a mode can be started on any degree by renumbering the cycle.
* Patterns work from any starting string and interlock across the neck.
* Practice: play all seven major keys that contain a chosen note, then their relative minors, from that note; play one scale in every position; improvise with other players, changing keys and modes and covering the whole neck.
* Scope limit stated by the author: harmonic and melodic minor are **not** covered, because they are altered scales.

**Verification.** The academy's theory engine re-derives the shape cycle from interval arithmetic (X = a 3NPS group starting on degree 1, 4 or 5; Y = degree 7 or 3; Z = degree 6 or 2), and a unit test confirms that Pillitiere's rules reproduce the computed 3NPS fingerings in every key and from strings 6, 5 and 4 in standard tuning. The rule-based "Pattern Builder" in the Scale Explorer is therefore a faithful, tested implementation rather than an approximation. Because rule 2 encodes standard tuning's major third between strings 3 and 2, the builder generalises it to "whenever the next string is tuned a major third higher" for other tunings, and labels that as an extension of the source.

## 4. Prerequisite graph (key dependencies)

```
tab + chord diagrams ─┬─> open chords ─> chord changes ─> barre chords (E/A shapes) ─> 7th, m7, sus/add barres
                      └─> single notes ─> alternate picking ─> riffs ─> power chords + palm muting
musical alphabet ─> half/whole steps ─> notes on the neck ─> octave shapes ─> intervals
intervals ─> major scale formula ─> scale degrees ─┬─> relative minor ─> natural minor
                                                  ├─> triads ─> diatonic chords ─> Roman numerals ─> functional harmony
                                                  └─> 3NPS shapes (Pillitiere X/Y/Z) ─> all 7 patterns ─> modes
minor pentatonic ─> blues scale ─> bends/vibrato ─> 12-bar blues improvisation ─> call and response
chord tones + arpeggios + scales ─> chord-tone targeting ─> improvising over changes
alternate picking (synchronised, clean at 100+ bpm 16ths) + arpeggio knowledge ─> sweep picking
```

## 6. Topics the brief requires that no supplied book covers

Electric guitar hardware (pickups, switching, amps, signal chain, set-up); full-neck note knowledge; intervals by name and by ear; the circle of fifths and key signatures; triad inversions, drop voicings, extended and altered chords; functional harmony and secondary dominants; CAGED; harmonic and melodic minor and their modes; symmetrical and exotic scales; every shred technique beyond trills; speed-development method; improvisation method; ear training; jamming and band skills; composition and transcription.

All of these are written from established guitar pedagogy and music theory and are marked **Supplementary** in the app.

## 7. How the app shows provenance

Every lesson carries a `sources` field. The lesson page shows a "Source basis" note naming the supplied book and section a concept comes from, or "Supplementary: established pedagogy" when no supplied book covers it. Song titles from the books are mentioned only as listening suggestions, never transcribed.
